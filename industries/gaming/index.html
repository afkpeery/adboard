Gaming / Campaign field notes

Your next launch belongs in the spotlight.

Give players a glimpse of the experience before asking them to join it. A distinctive character, a compelling world, or an unmistakable gameplay moment can communicate more than a crowded feature list.

Who you could reach

Players, stream viewers, independent game supporters, and communities exploring their next shared experience.

Build the creative around the game

Show what makes the experience recognizable. Keep a clear focal point and make the title readable at smaller sizes. If movement is part of the creative, provide a calm version for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

Choose one player action

Ask visitors to wishlist the game, watch a trailer, or join a playtest. Sending every placement to the same generic homepage makes it harder to understand which creative brings interested players. A focused destination creates a clearer journey.

Time the placement to a real milestone

A demo release, community event, or launch window gives the campaign a concrete story. Make platform availability, age guidance, and release timing easy to find on the linked page. Update those details when plans change.

Three ways to start

  • An indie-game wishlist campaign
  • A public playtest invitation
  • A creator-led community event
